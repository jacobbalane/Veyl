# Placement

Veyl hears your hand through echoes, so where the speaker and microphone sit matters more than
anything else. Start with the advice below, then watch the meter: it tells you, live, how well
the current setup works.

## Placing your phone

- **Lay it flat, screen up, in front of your monitor.** Your hand then moves above it while you
  look at the screen.
- **Keep the speaker and microphones uncovered.** On most phones they sit at the bottom edge, and
  often a second microphone at the top. A thick case over the holes, a hand resting on the phone
  or a stack of papers muffles the echoes.
- **Set the volume to about half or more.** Veyl needs the high sounds to reach your hand and
  come back.
- **Hold your hand 10–30 cm above it.** About 20 cm is a good start.
- **Leave the phone where it is.** When it moves, Veyl has to learn the room again ("Learning the
  room…"), which takes a moment.
- **Keep the screen on.** Veyl stops listening when the screen turns off. The listening screen dims
  itself; touch it to brighten it.

## Placing your microphone (PC mode)

- **Laptop:** hold your hand about 20 cm in front of the screen and keyboard, and poke toward the
  laptop.
- **Desktop:** keep the microphone within about 50 cm of where you poke, with speakers on and
  audible.
- Run **Check this PC's sound** in Settings after you move things. If it says the PC hears its
  sound poorly, move the microphone closer to where you poke, or turn the volume up.

In PC mode the **Distance range** starts at **Far**, because the hand is usually farther from
the microphone than from a phone.

## How to poke

A poke is a quick move toward the phone (or microphone) and back, like pressing a button in the
air.

- Start with your hand still, about 20 cm away.
- Move about 3 to 5 cm toward it and straight back, in well under a second. On the Normal
  setting Veyl needs at least about 3 cm toward it and 2 cm back.
- Wait a moment between pokes. Veyl ignores a second poke within about a third of a second.

Two settings change this:

| Setting        | Choices           | What it does                                                                              |
| -------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sensitivity    | Low, Normal, High | High catches smaller, slower pokes but may react to other movement; Low needs bigger ones |
| Distance range | Near, Normal, Far | How far away Veyl looks for a hand: about 25, 40 or 55 cm                                 |

Distances are echo distances: for a phone, about the distance from the phone. With the speaker
and microphone far apart, as on many desktops, they are smaller near the microphone; Veyl only
needs them to grow and shrink with your hand.

## What to avoid

- **Cases and covers** over the speaker or microphones. If the meter's **Clear echo** or
  **Volume** stays low, try without the case.
- **Cushions, beds and laps.** Soft surfaces swallow sound and the phone may shift. Use a desk or
  table.
- **Headphones.** The sound must come out of a speaker near your hand. With your phone, the sound
  must come from the phone's own speaker; in PC mode, from speakers rather than headphones or a
  headset.
- **Bluetooth.** In phone mode, sound going to Bluetooth headphones or a speaker doesn't work. In
  PC mode, Bluetooth speakers work but add delay; built-in or wired speakers work better.
- **Other sound near the microphone,** such as a TV, a second speaker or loud typing. Sounds that
  aren't part of the copy Veyl compares make the echo harder to read.
- **Other movement close by.** A second person, a pet or your other hand moving near the phone
  may look like a hand.
